## Y.1 Top-line findings

- Of **22 consent decrees, voluntary compliance agreements (VCAs), and settlements** with physical accessibility or accessible-unit components, **59.1% (13)** expressly required accessible-unit inventories, count verification, or registries.
- Strict express § 8.26 subset: **2 of 3** expressly required accessible-unit inventories AND occupancy/tenant-placement/tenant-matching tracking.
- Broader integration/distribution subset (treating Los Angeles's parallel Section 504 orders as separate instruments): **4 of 5** required occupancy or waiting-list tracking (3 of 4 if the parallel Los Angeles instruments are collapsed into one legal architecture).
- Lower-bound finding: courts and agencies have ordered accessible-unit inventories, count verification, registries, or unit-by-unit audits in the coded record at least 13 times. Including Olmstead placement-tracking systems and other non-inventory tracking architectures, the broader data-infrastructure count rises to **20**.

## Y.2 Coding conventions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Inventory** | Explicit accessible-unit inventory, count verification, registry, or unit-by-unit accessibility audit/order. |
| **Occupancy tracking** | Who occupies accessible units, or — in Olmstead matters — voucher utilization / placement / waiting-list tracking. |
| **Distribution** | Express § 8.26 dispersal term or broader integration/distribution architecture. |
| **Public registry** | Public-facing searchable registry, not an internal list. |

Conservative coding: unclear values stay unclear. Decree terms were not inferred from general practice alone.

## Y.8 Interpretation

The pattern is consistent across remedy types. DOJ design-and-construction decrees use inventories to make retrofit obligations administrable. Section 504 public-housing decrees layer on tenant placement, waiting-list, vacancy, and accommodation-request tracking whenever recipients must distribute accessible units or keep them occupied by people who need the features. Olmstead settlements do the same thing in functional form by counting supported-housing slots and tracking placements.

In other words: **inventory-and-tracking outputs are integration inputs**. When courts or agencies enforce integration/distribution duties in the coded record, the remedy formalizes inventory-and-tracking architecture. The Note's proposed certification-and-reporting framework is therefore not a novel administrative imposition; it is the institutional regularization of what consent-decree law already requires whenever it reaches the integration question.

## Y.9 Conservative-coding caveats

- The 13-case lower bound is deliberately conservative. Philadelphia PHA, PRPHA, Cambridge, and Atlanta were recoded from inventory = yes to inventory = unclear because the source memos describe unit targets, waiting-list duties, or generic tracking language without quoting a separate inventory/count-verification term.
- Quality Built, Design Professionals, Wallace, Chicopee, and ADAPT may have additional inventory terms; the source memos do not spell them out, so they were not counted upward.
- The broad 80.0% integration/distribution statistic treats the 2016 and 2019 Los Angeles instruments as separate matters; collapsing them yields 75.0%.
- NYCHA was excluded because the local record identifies only multiple informal monitoring arrangements, not one discrete decree/VCA.
- *Thompson v. HUD* was excluded from the accessibility denominator because it is a desegregation/mobility case rather than an accessible-unit remedy.
