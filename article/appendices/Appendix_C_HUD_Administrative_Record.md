# Appendix C — HUD Administrative Record: Form HUD-27061 and 24 C.F.R. Part 121

**PURPOSE.** This appendix assembles the complete, source-hashed administrative record that the
Note's Part I.E and Part IV summarize: the statutory and regulatory foundation for HUD disability
data collection (1988-1989), the 2003-2023 Paperwork Reduction Act (PRA) clearance cycles for Form
HUD-27061 (OMB Control No. 2535-0113), the 2022 protected-class-data proposal and its 2023
narrowing to race and ethnicity, the June 2026 renewal notice and June 30, 2026 approval
expiration, the parallel Fair Housing Initiatives Program (FHIP) litigation docket, and the
author's 2026 PRA comment as filed. Its function is evidentiary and archival: every fact
the manuscript states about this record traces to a primary document preserved in `record/hud-27061/`
and verified by SHA-256 hash. It states no legal conclusions; the legal argument is in the Note.
So that a reader can always tell evidence from advocacy, the appendix is organized in two labeled
parts: the public record (§§ C.1-C.3), which is assembled entirely from government-issued primary
sources and does not depend on anything the author did; and the author's participation and analysis
(§§ C.4-C.6), which records the author's own comment, the model petition templates, and the
supporting doctrinal analysis. Section numbering is unchanged from earlier printings; the
manuscript's citations to §§ C.1-C.6 resolve unaffected.

**PROVENANCE.** Primary sources harvested into `record/hud-27061/` (federal-government-issued
documents from the public record: Federal Register notices, OMB/OIRA ICR records and Supporting
Statements, HUD forms, GAO and OIG reports). Integrity manifest with SHA-256 hashes and source
URLs: `record/hud-27061/file_inventory.csv`. Longitudinal notice/cycle dataset:
`record/hud-27061/longitudinal_tables.csv`. The chronology below sets out, notice by notice, the documentary basis for the Note's account of the 2022-2026 Form HUD-27061 sequence (the manuscript cites this appendix at fn 115).

**EVIDENTIARY STATUS.** Relied on by the Note for the administrative-record (non-classification)
claims listed below; every quotation is Tier 1 (verbatim from a source binary, verified in
`record/hud-27061/quote_verification_report.md`). The RegInfo OMB-disposition line for the June 30,
2026 expiration is provisional as of the July 1, 2026 pull; see § C.1 for the current-status note.

**REGENERATION.** Not script-generated. Source binaries and their hashes are in
`record/hud-27061/`; to re-verify a quotation, extract the cited binary (`FRN60_2023.pdf`,

approval's June 30, 2026 expiration date had passed with no extension request, successor ICR, or

expiration and been reinstated before (the 2019 approval carried a July 31, 2022 expiration, and

identify respondent burden as the disclosed reason for narrowing, and that the 2026 renewal
instrument is, by HUD's own burden accounting, unchanged from the race-and-ethnicity collection.

### C.2 The 2023 Supporting Statement (Item 8) and terms of clearance

**Supporting Statement A, Item 8 (verbatim excerpt).** The 2023 Supporting Statement recorded that
three comments were received on the 2022 60-day notice and that "none caused a change in the form."
On the one substantive comment (a request to add sexual-orientation and gender-identity fields),
HUD wrote:

> "HUD appreciates the suggestions of the comment and remains interested in collecting SOGI data to
> inform enforcement of civil right statutes under HUD's jurisdiction. However, HUD is not adding
> the SOGI questions during this renewal process, which applies only to Form 27061. ... In
> particular, when Form 27061-H undergoes PRA renewal, among other HUD forms, HUD will evaluate
> inclusion of SOGI data collection, among other protected characteristics, to inform its
> grantmaking and oversight."

Source: `record/hud-27061/SSA_2023.txt`, Item 8 (extracted from `SSA_2023.docx` via python-docx;
SHA-256 in `file_inventory.csv`). The phrase "among other protected characteristics" is the
representation the Note's footnote 153 and the author's PRA comment reference: HUD stated on the
record that at future renewals it would evaluate inclusion of additional protected characteristics,
and the 2026 renewal notice reflects no such evaluation for the Part 121 disability ("handicap")
category.

**2023 terms of clearance (verbatim excerpt).** OIRA's approval carried terms of clearance
directed at data quality, not collection scope:

> "The agency is instructed that when using this data for public or Congressional reports, it should
> acknowledge certain limitations to this dataset. These include: (1) the inability for respondents
> to record non-responses or refused response data ...; (2) potentially varied collection methods,
> including both self-identification and proxy reporting; (3) that entities do not report
> multiracial categories that are below 1% of all respondents ... . No later than the next
> triannual renewal process or the next substantive revision in response to prospective updates to
> OMB's Statistical Policy Directive 15, the agency will undertake further revisions of this form
> to achieve the following: (1) Allow for the reporting of non-response or refused response data;
> (2) Provide more standardized direction to entities on the use of self-identification and/or
> proxy reporting ...; (3) using an alternative to Word processing software ...; (4) accommodating
> multi-racial reporting categories below 1%."

Source: `record/hud-27061/icr_202301-2535-001.html` (Terms of Clearance block).

**Neutral characterization.** The 2023 terms of clearance are data-quality conditions (non-response
handling, self-identification versus proxy reporting, multiracial-category suppression, and a
future move off word-processing software), keyed to the race-and-ethnicity fields the form
collects. They are not scope conditions; they do not direct HUD to add disability or any other
protected-class category. The relevance of the terms to the Note is therefore limited: an unchanged
renewal would leave the 2023 data-quality revisions unexecuted, but the terms are not authority for
the disability-data collection the Note proposes. (This characterization corresponds to the
manuscript's decision not to rest any scope argument on the terms of clearance.)

### C.6 ADAPT/Chaney and the PRA § 3507(d)(6) distinction (extended)

This section carries the relocated detail from the Note's footnotes 118 and 122 that bears on why
the PRA does not preclude the review the petition contemplates. The standing- and
reviewability-facing ADAPT analysis is developed further in Appendix D; this section addresses the
PRA-preclusion and *Chaney* points specifically.

**The § 3507(d)(6) bar reaches OMB clearance, not petition denial.** 44 U.S.C. § 3507(d)(6)
provides that the OMB Director's decision to approve or disapprove an agency information collection
is not subject to judicial review. That bar insulates OMB's clearance decisions on Form
HUD-27061's ICR. It does not insulate HUD's denial of a § 553(e) petition for a disability-data
rule, which is a separate agency action by a different agency (HUD, not OMB) directed at a
different object (whether to initiate rulemaking under HUD's own statutory and regulatory
authorities, not whether to approve a collection). The Note therefore does not rely on direct
*State Farm* review of the 2023 information-collection narrowing, which would raise finality,
defendant-identity, PRA-preclusion (44 U.S.C. § 3507(d)(6)), and mootness questions; it relies on
review of a § 553(e) denial, to which the § 3507(d)(6) bar does not extend. (The full
contemporaneous-rationale point under *SEC v. Chenery* is in the manuscript.)

**Why *ADAPT* does not control on the *Chaney* axis.** In *ADAPT v. HUD*, the Third Circuit
rejected APA review of a generalized effort to compel HUD disability enforcement, holding that HUD
"has no affirmative duty under any statute or regulation to initiate nationwide investigation and
prosecution," 170 F.3d at 386 — language addressed to enforcement discretion under *Heckler v.
Chaney*, not to recordkeeping or to a discrete rulemaking petition. ADAPT pressed § 3608(e)(6)
and, below, Part 121 only as "law to apply" against discretionary enforcement; the Third Circuit
read § 3608(e)(6) as concerning data on those who benefit or seek to benefit from HUD programs
(the person-level scope this Note adopts), 170 F.3d at 387 n.9, and § 3614a was never invoked. The
district court's one-sentence rejection of Part 121 misdescribed the regulation as implementing
the FHAA's accessibility requirements; neither opinion engaged Part 121's actual text. Because the
present claim targets a discrete rulemaking petition grounded in § 121.2, § 3608(e)(6), and
§ 3614a, and rests on a 2022-2026 PRA record that did not exist in 1998, it is distinguishable on
the enforcement-versus-rulemaking axis *Chaney* and *Massachusetts v. EPA* draw. (The standing
and § 3604(f)(5)(D) points are developed in Appendix D.)
