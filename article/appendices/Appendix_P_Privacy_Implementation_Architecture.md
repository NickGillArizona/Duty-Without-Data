# Appendix P — Privacy and Implementation Architecture (Extended Comparator Catalogue)

**Role.** This appendix is the extended privacy-and-implementation comparator
catalogue that the manuscript's Part III.B summarizes and that manuscript
footnotes 104, 106, and 162 cite. It addresses requester-facing records, the Part 8
promulgation history, and comparable federal recordkeeping regimes. The manuscript
keeps each proposition and its representative authorities on-page (the Los Angeles
Voluntary Compliance Agreement and the Greenbriar letter of findings and compliance
agreement remain printed as the feasibility sources of record); the catalogue below
carries the full citation detail verbatim. The extended analyses underlying this
catalogue — privacy taxonomy, tiered disclosure, assisted-housing phasing, and the
HMDA / § 3614a public-versus-restricted analogy — are preserved in the project's
research records (see `replication/DATA_PROVENANCE.md` on privately retained
material).

## P.1 Requester-facing record comparators (cited by fn 162)

Regulation B requires adverse-action notification with a statement of specific
reasons. 12 C.F.R. § 1002.9(a)(1)–(2) (2026). IDEA requires prior written notice.
34 C.F.R. § 300.503(a)–(b) (2026). Municipal law supplies a housing analog: New
York City requires "a written final determination identifying any accommodation
granted or denied," N.Y.C. Admin. Code § 8-107(28)(c)–(d), while California's
detailed interactive-process regulations impose no comparable duty, see Cal. Code
Regs. tit. 2, §§ 12176–12185. The written outcome is administrable at municipal
scale, and even the most detailed state process regime stops short of requiring
the record. Each comparator shows a requester-facing record supplementing
substantive rights without becoming an element of the claim.

## P.2 Part 8 promulgation history and the HHS Part 84 comparator (cited by fn 104)

24 C.F.R. Part 8 was originally promulgated as Nondiscrimination Based on
Handicap In Federally Assisted Programs and Activities of the Department of
Housing and Urban Development, 53 Fed. Reg. 20,216, 20,233 (June 2, 1988), and
has never been comprehensively revised — only section-level amendments, e.g., 89
Fed. Reg. 38,224, 38,290 (May 7, 2024) (amending § 8.28). Compare
Nondiscrimination on the Basis of Disability in Programs or Activities Receiving
Federal Financial Assistance, 89 Fed. Reg. 40,066 (May 9, 2024) (the HHS Part 84
update — the first comprehensive § 504 revision in a generation; after the June
2026 OLC opinion, administrability evidence still, durability evidence no
longer).

## P.3 Recurring federal § 504 collection comparators (cited by fn 106)

Recurring disability-compliance collection is administrable at scale across
comparable federal § 504 agencies: the Civil Rights Data Collection, 34 C.F.R.
§ 100.6(b) (2026); the National Transit Database, 49 U.S.C. § 5335; and the
CC-305 voluntary self-identification collection, 41 C.F.R. § 60-741.42 (2026).
The comparators are administrability evidence, not interpretive authority.
