## 2. The durable vehicle: the § 553(e) petition

The comment window closes; the petition does not. Under 5 U.S.C. § 553(e), any interested person may
petition HUD to issue or amend a rule. Filing the petition triggers HUD's duty to conclude the matter
within a reasonable time (5 U.S.C. § 555(b)) and to give a statement of grounds for any denial
(§ 555(e)) — and a denial is judicially reviewable under *Massachusetts v. EPA*, 549 U.S. 497, 527–28
(2007). That is the Note's proposed remedy: not a lawsuit first, but a petition that obligates HUD to
answer, on the record, whether continued non-engagement with its own disability-data authority is
still reasoned.

Two variants are drafted:

- **Organizational:** [`action/553e_petition_template.md`](553e_petition_template.md) —
  for a state Protection and Advocacy agency, a fair-housing organization, or a comparable
  "interested person" whose programmatic work depends on the missing data.
- **Individual:** [`action/553e_petition_individual.md`](553e_petition_individual.md) —
  for a disabled HUD-program participant or applicant. The template explains why this is the
  lower-risk vehicle for an individual: § 553(e)'s "interested person" standard is materially broader
  than Article III standing, so a person who could not plead a full federal case can still petition,
  build the record, and create reviewable agency action.

Mechanics, from the templates: HUD maintains no dedicated petition portal, so submit by certified
mail (return receipt requested) to the Office of the Secretary, with a copy to HUD's Office of
General Counsel by email with confirmed receipt, and optionally file the petition document on the
open regulations.gov docket, clearly titled "Petition for Rulemaking — 5 U.S.C. § 553(e)." Then keep
the calendar: a written status follow-up at 60 days, a second follow-up citing § 555(b) at 180 days,
and at one year a documented delay record for counsel to evaluate under the *TRAC* factors — no
fixed period establishes unreasonable delay; agency priorities, complexity, competing demands, and
prejudice all bear on it.

## 3. Only if HUD denies without engagement: the judicial-review layer

The petition's purpose is met when HUD engages it on the merits. **No litigation template is
provided** for the other branch: any complaint would need to respond to an actual agency
disposition, an identified plaintiff, the governing venue, and the resulting administrative
record — none of which presently exists. Judicial-review materials, if that contingency ever
arises, must be developed by counsel from the actual disposition.

The standing realities, after *FDA v. Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine*, 602 U.S. 367 (2024), and
*Connecticut Fair Housing Center v. CoreLogic Rental Property Solutions, LLC*, 167 F.4th 605 (2d Cir.
2026): lead with an individual whose assisted-housing transaction is live — a voucher holder,
waitlist applicant, or tenant with an open accommodation, transfer, or extension request.
Organizational plaintiffs reinforce rather than carry the case, and diversion-of-resources theories
of organizational standing are contraindicated. The governing analysis is the manuscript's Part IV and the extended annex at
[`article/appendices/Appendix_D_Standing_Reviewability_Annex.md`](../article/appendices/Appendix_D_Standing_Reviewability_Annex.md).

## 4. Using the data: what to cite, and what not to cite

Advocates can safely cite the Note's **ledger-backed findings**: the Note's printed and directly
relied-on claims each map to a source and an evidence route in
[`article/CLAIMS_LEDGER.csv`](../article/CLAIMS_LEDGER.csv) — 53 rows, every route checked at release
by [`scripts/check_claims_ledger.py`](../scripts/check_claims_ledger.py) — the
figures regenerate from the canonical artifacts (see [`replication/REPRODUCE.md`](../replication/REPRODUCE.md)), and the
extended analyses live in [`article/appendices/`](../article/appendices/). Two limits worth knowing
before you cite: 41 registered assertions are recomputed automatically, which is a selection
rather than every number in the article; and 12 of the 53 rows rest on the cited primary source
plus privately retained material, so verify those at the primary source itself.

Exploratory research memoranda are **not published in this archive** and are not citable
authority: the LLM-assisted working analyses that once accompanied the results layer are
retained in the project's private research records (see
[`replication/DATA_PROVENANCE.md`](../replication/DATA_PROVENANCE.md)), and their negative and
superseded results are summarized in
[`article/EVIDENCE_AND_LIMITS.md`](../article/EVIDENCE_AND_LIMITS.md). The published
boundary is simple: ledger-backed findings and cited appendices are citable; nothing else is.
