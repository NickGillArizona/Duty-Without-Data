## 2. The 2022–2023 history is a central, unresolved aspect of this renewal

2.1. On September 27, 2022, HUD published a 60-day notice proposing to update Form HUD-27061 "to collect protected class data as required by the Fair Housing Act and HUD regulations at 24 CFR 121." 87 Fed. Reg. 58524, 58525 (Sept. 27, 2022).

2.2. On January 27, 2023, HUD published a 30-day notice that silently reverted to a race-and-ethnicity-only scope, without any textual acknowledgment of the proposed expansion, the Part 121 invocation, or the Fair Housing Act basis stated in the 60-day notice. 88 Fed. Reg. 5370 (Jan. 27, 2023).

2.3. HUD's Supporting Statement submitted to OIRA in connection with the 2023 renewal (cycle reference 202301-2535-001) explained the "Program Changes or Adjustments" from the previously approved collection by reference only to a clerical field relabeling (DUNS to UEI) and a PRA-statement update. The Supporting Statement did not address the abandonment of the proposed Part 121 expansion.

2.4. OIRA approved the narrowed 2023 ICR on June 13, 2023, without any public-record explanation for the abandonment of the proposed expansion.

2.5. The public record for the 2022–2023 cycle therefore contains no agency statement of reasons for the abandonment. That silence is unresolved as HUD undertakes the 2026 renewal. This comment asks HUD to resolve it on the record in the 2026 submission.

2.6. The June 12, 2026 notice proposes to renew the narrowed collection while continuing to describe it as collecting "other protected class data . . . as required by . . . the Fair Housing Act." 91 Fed. Reg. at 35,698. The description and the fielded instrument therefore remain divergent in the 2026 cycle, and the burden estimate is unchanged from 2022 and 2023, which forecloses respondent burden as the disclosed reason for the narrower scope.

2.7. The most recent approval for this collection stated an expiration date of June 30, 2026. That date has passed while this renewal remains pending, and no OMB disposition has posted. Whether OMB renews the collection or the expiration goes unrepaired, HUD must supply the same explanation on the record: a renewal approved unchanged re-ratifies the 2023 omission in a fresh 2026 PRA record, and an unrepaired expiration leaves the enacted duties this comment identifies with no approved cross-program collection. [COMMENTER ORGANIZATION] takes no position on which disposition will issue and asks HUD to address the merits of the omission either way.

## 3. Specific requests

For the 2026 final submission and 30-day notice, [COMMENTER ORGANIZATION] respectfully requests that HUD:

### 3.1. Address the 2022 proposed expansion on the record

HUD should expressly address, in the 2026 Supporting Statement (Questions 1, 2, 8, and 15) and in the 2026 30-day Federal Register notice, whether the Secretary has determined that collection of protected-class data beyond race and ethnicity is necessary or appropriate under 42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6) and 24 CFR Part 121. If the Secretary has determined such collection is not necessary or appropriate, HUD should state the reasons for that determination.

### 3.2. Explain the abandonment

HUD should explain, on the record, why the expansion proposed in the 2022 60-day notice — to "collect protected class data as required by the Fair Housing Act and HUD regulations at 24 CFR 121" — was abandoned between the 60-day notice and the 30-day submission. "Explanation" here means a statement sufficient to satisfy the APA's reasoned-decisionmaking standard under *FCC v. Fox Television Stations, Inc.*, 556 U.S. 502 (2009), and *Motor Vehicle Mfrs. Ass'n v. State Farm*, 463 U.S. 29 (1983), which require an agency changing position to display awareness of the change and to provide good reasons for the new position.

### 3.3. Address 42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6) specifically

The 2023 Supporting Statement quoted § 3608(e)(6) in full, including its direction that HUD "collect[] and annually report[] . . . data on the race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, handicap, and family characteristics" of persons served by HUD programs, but did not explain why the authorized collection of "handicap" and "family characteristics" data is not implemented through Form HUD-27061. HUD should address this question on the record in 2026.

### 3.4. Address 24 CFR Part 121 specifically

Current 24 CFR Part 121, titled "Collection of Data," directs HUD program participants to furnish data on the categories enumerated in § 3608(e)(6), "as the Secretary determines is necessary or appropriate," to carry out HUD's responsibilities under the Fair Housing Act and related statutes. HUD's 2022 60-day notice invoked Part 121 as regulatory authority for the proposed expansion. HUD should state, on the record in 2026, the Secretary's current determination under Part 121 and the reasons supporting that determination.

### 3.5. Address 24 CFR Part 8 and Section 504

HUD's disability-nondiscrimination regime at 24 CFR Part 8 (implementing Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act) and its Section 504 self-evaluation obligations at 24 CFR § 8.51 presuppose the availability of disability-status data from HUD-assisted programs. HUD should explain, on the record, how Section 504 compliance and monitoring is achieved without a standardized HUD-level disability-data collection instrument.

### 3.6. Address privacy-preserving data-collection alternatives

If HUD's basis for declining to collect protected-class data beyond race and ethnicity is a privacy concern, HUD should state that basis expressly and should address privacy-preserving alternatives the agency has considered, including (but not limited to):

- response-level aggregation (program-level or ZIP-level rollups rather than individual records);
- opt-in collection with explicit consent;
- differential-privacy or other statistical-disclosure-limitation methods;
- separate disability / family-characteristics questions that do not link to the individual grant record.

### 3.7. Address burden with actual proposed fields

HUD's 2022 60-day notice and 2023 30-day notice contained identical burden estimates (14,375 respondents; 1.2 frequency; 0.50 hours per response; 8,625 total annual burden hours). If HUD's 2026 submission proposes a narrower scope than the 2022 proposal, HUD should explain whether the 2022 proposal's respondent-burden estimate has been reassessed and what fields were contemplated in the 2022 proposal that are not contemplated in 2026.

### 3.8. Respond to the 2022 SAGE/Jones Day comment's Part 121 argument

The only substantive public comment received during the 2022 60-day window, from the law firm Jones Day on behalf of Services & Advocacy for LGBT Elders (Regulations.gov ID HUD-2006-0214-0008), invoked 24 CFR §§ 121.1 and 121.2 and argued that HUD is required by law to collect additional data thereunder. HUD's 2023 Supporting Statement responded to the SOGI aspect of that comment but did not respond to the comment's Part 121 statutory-authority argument. HUD should respond to that argument on the record in 2026.
