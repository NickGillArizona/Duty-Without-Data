# Adaptation Checklist

Every document in this directory is a research template for counsel's independent
review and adaptation. These are research materials, not legal advice -- neither the
templates nor this checklist -- and no template is ready for filing without
case-specific review by a licensed attorney. This checklist is the
adaptation procedure for all of them; the templates themselves carry only final
text, not drafting instructions.

Part A applies to every document in the kit. Part B collects the terms that attach
to a single document.

---

# Part A — Applies to every document

## 1. Confirm the agency posture -- do not assume it

The templates address the information collection approved under OMB Control Number
2535-0113 (Form HUD-27061). Its status can change at any time. Before adapting any
document, check the current record yourself:

- RegInfo (reginfo.gov): the OMB control number's current status and any concluded
  review.
- Regulations.gov: the renewal docket, its comment deadline, and any posted
  disposition.
- The Federal Register: any notice published after this repository's record cutoff.

As of the archived record cutoff of July 8, 2026, HUD's June 12, 2026 renewal notice
remains pending and OMB has posted no disposition; the collection's displayed
expiration date of June 30, 2026 has passed. Until a disposition posts, filings
should address both possibilities -- renewal and lapse -- and assert neither as
accomplished. Do not state that the collection has lapsed, or that it has been
renewed, unless the official record you check says so.

## 2. Confirm the docket and the deadline

The renewal notice is the 60-Day Notice of Proposed Information Collection published
at 91 Fed. Reg. 35,697 (June 12, 2026), FR Doc. 2026-11891, Docket No. FR-7093-N-02,
on regulations.gov Docket ID HUD-2006-0214 (comment document HUD-2006-0214-0010).
The comment window this repository's materials were built against closes Tuesday,
August 11, 2026. Verify the docket number and comment deadline on Regulations.gov
before filing anything; deadlines can be extended or reopened.

## 3. Replace every user-input field

Each template marks the fields the filer must supply (name, organization,
signature block, contact information, and any facts specific to the filer).
Search each document for bracketed fields before treating it as complete.

## 4. Recheck every authority

Statutes, regulations, and cases cited in the templates were verified against the
sources current when the templates were prepared. Shepardize or KeyCite every
authority before filing, and verify every Federal Register citation against the
published page. In the hyperlinked complaint, re-verify every URL; eCFR URLs in
particular change as the CFR is updated.

## 5. Remove private information

Declarations and complaints invite facts about disability and housing. Before
filing or sharing a draft, remove or generalize medical details that are not
necessary to the legal theory, and confirm the filer understands that filed
documents are public records. Redact account numbers, addresses beyond what venue
requires, and third-party names that are not parties.

## 6. Jurisdiction and venue (complaint only)

Confirm the correct district, division, and venue basis for the filer's facts, and
complete the jurisdiction and venue section accordingly. The model section states
the general federal-question basis; local rules and standing orders vary.

## 7. Standing (petition and complaint)

The standing sections plead informational and organizational injury frameworks.
Confirm the filer's actual injury fits the framework pleaded, and conform the
allegations to the filer's real activities, expenditures, and record requests.

## 8. Exhibits and record integrity

If the filing attaches records from this repository's administrative-record
archive, cite the archived copies and their hashes as published, and state the
date retrieved. If counsel re-collects any source, recompute hashes for the
re-collected copies and cite those instead. Exhibit numbering in the templates is
illustrative; renumber to the filing's own exhibit set.

## 9. Signature, service, and format

Confirm signature block requirements, service rules, page limits, and formatting
under the forum's local rules. Comment filings on Regulations.gov have their own
attachment and character constraints; verify before pasting.

## 10. Archive what you file

Keep the as-filed version, its confirmation receipt, and the exhibits as a
separate, unmodified record. Do not overwrite the template with the filed
version.

# Part B — Document-specific terms

## B.1 Public comment (`2026_comment_template.md`)

- **Quantify the workaround costs.** Paragraph 1.3's quantification of the
  organization's non-litigation costs to work around the absence of HUD-collected
  data is the single most important litigation-preparation element. Gather and
  retain documentation of those costs before filing.
- **Record-preservation value.** Even if HUD's 2026 final submission narrows scope
  again, a comment of this specificity satisfies the significant-comment threshold
  under *United States v. Nova Scotia Food Products Corp.*, 568 F.2d 240, 252 (2d
  Cir. 1977), and *Portland Cement Ass'n v. Ruckelshaus*, 486 F.2d 375 (D.C. Cir.
  1973), such that a failure to respond would independently be arbitrary and
  capricious.
- **Parallel petition.** Consider filing the section 553(e) petition at
  `./553e_petition_template.md` in parallel. The two vehicles are complementary,
  not duplicative.
- **Portal versus email.** Regulations.gov submissions are time-stamped and
  auto-docketed. Email submissions to a HUD address may or may not be auto-docketed;
  if email is chosen, request written confirmation of receipt and docket inclusion.
- **Multi-signatory comment.** Consider circulating the template for additional
  organizational signatories. A joint comment from three to five organizations with
  distinct programmatic profiles substantially strengthens the administrative record.

## B.2 Section 553(e) petitions (`553e_petition_template.md`, `553e_petition_individual.md`)

- **2026 legal-landscape discipline.** The individual petition's Sections 5.7-5.8
  and 9 describe a fast-moving landscape. Before filing: (a) verify the EEOC
  proposal's Federal Register citation, status, and covered report series against
  the published text -- do not rely on secondary descriptions; (b) never characterize
  *Olmstead* as overruled, 24 CFR 8.4(d) as rescinded, the integration mandate as
  gone, or the EEO reports as eliminated while the rescission remains proposed;
  (c) re-check whether HUD, HHS, or DOJ has taken further action on the integration
  regulations, and conform those sections to the record as of the filing date.
- **Privacy sweep.** Review the entire document for personally identifiable
  information that is not necessary for the petition to function. The petitioner's
  name, address, general disability category, and general HUD-program history are
  necessary. Medical-record specifics, case numbers, and account numbers belong in a
  separately retained exhibit, not the petition body.
- **Calendar the follow-ups.** At 60 days, send a written follow-up requesting
  status. At 180 days, send a second follow-up citing 5 U.S.C. 555(b). At one year,
  the unreasonable-delay record is well established; consult counsel on whether to
  pursue section 706(1) relief.
- **Reviewability.** An explicit denial becomes final agency action under
  *Massachusetts v. EPA*, 549 U.S. 497 (2007). A denial to an individual disabled
  petitioner produces a reviewable action without requiring the petitioner to plead
  an Article III case for the underlying HUD-27061 reversion itself.
- **Organizational and individual filings.** An individual and an organization may
  file parallel petitions; the two produce two independent section 555(b) /
  section 706(1) records. Coordinating filers should cross-reference each other's
  petitions. The organizational template's requested relief should be conformed to
  the four-module minimum-record architecture stated in the individual template
  (Sections 5.7-5.8, 6.1, and 9) before filing.
- **Supporting FOIA request.** A separate FOIA request to HUD PD&R for
  disability-disaggregated HUD-27061 data, filed in advance of the petition,
  strengthens the factual predicate. An incomplete or non-responsive FOIA response
  is documented informational injury and can be attached as an exhibit.
- **Copy to OIRA.** Send a copy to OMB/OIRA so the petition appears in both
  administrative records.
- **Counsel review.** An individual petitioner should have the document reviewed by
  counsel with administrative-law or disability-rights experience before filing,
  particularly to confirm that the disclosed facts are accurate and complete, that
  the privacy pass is adequate, that the reservation of rights does not conflict
  with any pending matter, and that the petitioner understands the downstream
  litigation posture the petition creates.

## B.3 Judicial-review materials (none published)

No complaint or declaration template is published in this archive. Any judicial-review
filing would need to respond to an actual agency disposition, an identified plaintiff,
the governing venue, and the resulting administrative record — none of which presently
exists — and must be developed by counsel from the actual disposition. The archived
primary-record files in [`../record/hud-27061/`](../record/hud-27061/) (with the
integrity manifest at `file_inventory.csv`) are the record materials such a filing would
authenticate.
- **Remedy framing.** Remand without vacatur is the remedy the template requests. If
  the plaintiff's circumstances support vacatur, reconsider the framing -- and
  conform it to the posture of the pending renewal as of the filing date.
- **Link hygiene.** In the hyperlinked version, re-verify every URL, consider
  stripping inline URLs from filed papers (most district courts prefer traditional
  citations), and retain the hyperlinked version as a research reference.
