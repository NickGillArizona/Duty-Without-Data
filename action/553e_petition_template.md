# 5 U.S.C. § 553(e) Petition Template — Form HUD-27061 / OMB Control No. 2535-0113

**Document type:** Template for adaptation by a petitioner. Not legal advice; independent review by counsel is required before use. The adaptation procedure is [`./ADAPTATION_CHECKLIST.md`](./ADAPTATION_CHECKLIST.md).

**Note to organizational filers (current through July 21, 2026).** The architecture of the requested relief is the four-module defined minimum-record rule stated in the companion individual template (`./553e_petition_individual.md`, Sections 5.7–5.8, 6.1, and 9): transaction records with a requester copy; accessible-unit asset records; § 3608(e)(6) aggregate reporting; and a severable, regulator-only matching module. An organizational filer should adapt those sections rather than rely on the form-modification requests below alone, and should carry the same 2026 legal-landscape material (the June 18, 2026 OLC opinion; the July 20, 2026 DOJ *Olmstead*-guidance notice, 91 Fed. Reg. 45,287; and the EEOC's proposed rescission of the EEO reports, RIN 3046-AB37 — verify status at filing) into the factual basis and administrative-record request. Note also that the companion Note's standing analysis now favors an individual lead petitioner for any follow-on judicial review after *FDA v. Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine*; this organizational template is best used as the parallel-filing vehicle contemplated in the individual template's notes, with the organization's role focused on the administrative record rather than litigation lead.

**Purpose.** To file a petition under 5 U.S.C. § 553(e) asking HUD to initiate rulemaking or to modify the approved Information Collection Request for OMB Control Number 2535-0113 (Form HUD-27061) to include protected-class data collection under 24 CFR Part 121 and 42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6). The vehicle is complementary to, not a substitute for, the 2026 60-day comment (see `./2026_comment_template.md`).

**Why use this in addition to the comment.** A § 553(e) petition triggers a distinct agency obligation: to respond within a "reasonable time." 5 U.S.C. § 555(b). An unreasonable delay in responding is itself reviewable under 5 U.S.C. § 706(1) (compel agency action unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed). The petition therefore creates a second litigation vehicle independent of the PRA renewal schedule.

**Author status.** Drafted by a JD candidate, not by an attorney. Before submission, the petitioner should (a) customize sections 1 and 6 to the petitioner's programmatic profile; (b) sign and submit; (c) calendar follow-up dates (60 days, 180 days, 1 year from submission) to establish an unreasonable-delay record.

**Submission path.** HUD does not maintain a dedicated § 553(e) petition portal. Submit by:

- Primary: certified mail, return receipt requested, to the Office of the Secretary.
- Secondary: email to the General Counsel's office with confirmed receipt.
- Tertiary: Regulations.gov, filed under a relevant open docket (HUD-2006-0214 continuing) with the petition document clearly titled "Petition for Rulemaking — 5 U.S.C. § 553(e)."

## 3. Legal framework

3.1. **42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6)** directs HUD to "collect[] and annually report[] to the Congress and make[] available to the public, data on the race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, handicap, and family characteristics" of persons and households "who are applicants for, participants in, or beneficiaries or potential beneficiaries of, programs administered by the Department."

3.2. **42 U.S.C. § 3614a** authorizes the Secretary to "make rules . . . to carry out" the Fair Housing Act.

3.3. **24 CFR Part 121**, titled "Collection of Data," directs HUD program participants to furnish data on the categories enumerated in § 3608(e)(6), "as the Secretary determines is necessary or appropriate" to carry out HUD's Fair Housing Act and other statutory responsibilities. 24 CFR §§ 121.1, 121.2.

3.4. **24 CFR Part 8** (implementing Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act) and HUD's self-evaluation obligation at 24 CFR § 8.51 presuppose the availability of disability-status data from HUD-assisted programs.

## 4. Factual basis

4.1. On September 27, 2022, HUD published a 60-day notice proposing to update Form HUD-27061 "to collect protected class data as required by the Fair Housing Act and HUD regulations at 24 CFR 121." 87 Fed. Reg. 58524, 58525.

4.2. On January 27, 2023, HUD published a 30-day notice that silently reverted to a race-and-ethnicity-only scope. 88 Fed. Reg. 5370.

4.3. The 2023 Supporting Statement submitted to OIRA explained the "Program Changes or Adjustments" from the previously approved collection solely by reference to a clerical field relabeling (DUNS to UEI) and a PRA-statement update. It did not address the abandonment of the proposed Part 121 / Fair Housing Act expansion.

4.4. OIRA approved the narrowed 2023 ICR on June 13, 2023, without any public-record explanation for the abandonment.

4.5. The Secretary has thus not stated, on the public record, a current determination under 24 CFR Part 121 as to whether data on disability, family characteristics, or other protected-class categories beyond race and ethnicity is "necessary or appropriate" for HUD to discharge its Fair Housing Act and related responsibilities.

## 5. Actions requested

Petitioner respectfully requests that the Secretary take one or more of the following actions:

### 5.1. Initiate rulemaking

Initiate a rulemaking under 5 U.S.C. § 553(b) to (a) amend 24 CFR Part 121 to specify the categories of data HUD will require of its program participants for civil-rights monitoring, or (b) amend HUD's Title VI and Fair Housing Act implementing regulations to formalize a broader standardized civil-rights data-collection instrument beyond Form HUD-27061's current race-and-ethnicity scope.

### 5.2. Modify the approved 2023 ICR

In the alternative, modify the approved 2023 ICR (202301-2535-001) under 44 U.S.C. § 3507(i), which permits OMB-approved modifications of approved collections, to expand Form HUD-27061 to include disability, family-characteristics, and other protected-class categories consistent with 42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6) and 24 CFR Part 121.

### 5.3. Issue a reasoned explanation on the record

In the further alternative, issue a written determination, published in the Federal Register, stating the Secretary's current determination under 24 CFR Part 121 as to whether collection of disability, family-characteristics, and other protected-class data is "necessary or appropriate" for HUD to discharge its Fair Housing Act and related responsibilities, and the Secretary's reasons for that determination. This alternative satisfies the reasoned-explanation obligation under *FCC v. Fox Television Stations, Inc.*, 556 U.S. 502 (2009), and *Motor Vehicle Mfrs. Ass'n v. State Farm*, 463 U.S. 29 (1983), for HUD's departure from the scope it had proposed in the 2022 60-day notice.

### 5.4. Respond within a reasonable time

In any event, under 5 U.S.C. § 555(b), respond to this Petition within a reasonable time — not to exceed 180 days from the date of submission — with a written decision granting, denying, or otherwise disposing of the Petition, including a statement of the grounds for the disposition.
