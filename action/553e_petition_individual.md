# 5 U.S.C. § 553(e) Petition — Individual Disabled Petitioner

**Document type:** Template for adaptation by an individual petitioner with a disability. Not legal advice; independent review by counsel is required before use. The adaptation procedure is [`./ADAPTATION_CHECKLIST.md`](./ADAPTATION_CHECKLIST.md).

**Current through:** July 21, 2026. The primary requested action is the four-module defined minimum-record rule (Section 6.1); the June–July 2026 federal actions are stated in the factual basis (Sections 5.7–5.8) and the administrative-record request (Section 8.3); severability and anticipated objections are addressed in Section 9.

**Relationship to `./553e_petition_template.md`.** The companion template is drafted for an organizational petitioner whose programmatic work is impaired by HUD's 2023 reversion. This template is drafted for an *individual* petitioner with a disability who has personally encountered, or is currently encountering, a HUD-assisted program. The two templates are complementary: one organizational and one individual vehicle, both producing the same § 555(b) / § 706(1) reviewable-action profile, may be filed in parallel by coordinating filers.

**Why § 553(e), for an individual, is the lower-risk vehicle.** § 553(e) grants "each agency shall give an interested person the right to petition for the issuance, amendment, or repeal of a rule." The "interested person" standard is materially broader than Article III standing. An individual who could not plead a fully surviving Article III case (because, for example, their HUD-program interaction is historical rather than current; or because the causal chain from the 2023 reversion to specific personal harm is not fully documented) may nevertheless be an "interested person" for § 553(e) purposes. The petition vehicle therefore enables an individual with a disability to participate in the administrative record and to create reviewable agency action (denial; unreasonable delay) without a conventional Article III posture.

**What the petition does not do.**

- It does not establish Article III standing for any future APA challenge filed by the petitioner.
- It does not create an attorney-client relationship between the petitioner and the drafter of this template.
- It does not replace counsel. A petitioner who intends to use this vehicle should consult counsel with administrative-law or disability-rights experience before filing, particularly if the petitioner contemplates follow-on litigation.

**Author status.** Drafted by a JD candidate, not by an attorney. Before submission, the petitioner should (a) customize sections 1, 2, and 7 to the petitioner's specific disability and HUD-program experience; (b) assemble the least identifying support sufficient for the filing's purpose (HUD-program records, correspondence, FOIA responses; medical documentation is not required for filing and should not be attached as a routine matter); (c) sign and submit; (d) calendar follow-up dates (60 days, 180 days, 1 year from submission) to establish an unreasonable-delay record.

**Submission paths.** HUD does not maintain a dedicated § 553(e) petition portal. Submit by:

- **Primary:** certified mail, return receipt requested, to the Office of the Secretary.
- **Secondary:** email to the Office of General Counsel with confirmed receipt.
- **Tertiary:** Regulations.gov, filed under a relevant open docket (HUD-2006-0214 continuing) with the petition document clearly titled "Petition for Rulemaking — 5 U.S.C. § 553(e)."

**Privacy.** This petition requires the petitioner to describe personal disability and housing facts. Petitioners should redact or omit medical-record specifics from the filing itself and retain documentation in a separate file for production if later requested. HUD publishes submitted petitions; assume the petition will be public.

## 4. Legal framework

4.1. **42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6)** directs HUD to "collect[] and annually report[] to the Congress and make[] available to the public, data on the race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, handicap, and family characteristics" of persons and households "who are applicants for, participants in, or beneficiaries or potential beneficiaries of, programs administered by the Department."

4.2. **42 U.S.C. § 3614a** authorizes the Secretary to "make rules . . . to carry out" the Fair Housing Act.

4.3. **24 CFR Part 121**, titled "Collection of Data," directs HUD program participants to furnish data on the categories enumerated in § 3608(e)(6), "as the Secretary determines is necessary or appropriate" to carry out HUD's Fair Housing Act and other statutory responsibilities. 24 CFR §§ 121.1, 121.2.

4.4. **24 CFR Part 8** (implementing Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act) requires:

- § 8.22 — new construction and substantial alteration of multi-family HUD-assisted projects to produce at least 5% of units accessible to persons with mobility impairments and 2% of units accessible to persons with sensory impairments;
- § 8.21(c)(4) — existing facilities to be covered by a transition plan identifying structural changes required for program accessibility;
- § 8.51 — recipient self-evaluation of § 504 compliance, including an inventory of policies and practices that do not comply.

Each of these obligations presupposes the availability of disability-status data on HUD-assisted populations. HUD's 2023 decision not to collect that data on Form HUD-27061 directly degrades the monitoring regime Part 8 contemplates and harms individuals with disabilities who rely on that regime to obtain accessible housing.

## 5. Factual basis

5.1. On September 27, 2022, HUD published a 60-day notice proposing to update Form HUD-27061 "to collect protected class data as required by the Fair Housing Act and HUD regulations at 24 CFR 121." 87 Fed. Reg. 58524, 58525.

5.2. On January 27, 2023, HUD published a 30-day notice that silently reverted to a race-and-ethnicity-only scope. 88 Fed. Reg. 5370.

5.3. The 2023 Supporting Statement submitted to OIRA explained the "Program Changes or Adjustments" from the previously approved collection solely by reference to a clerical field relabeling (DUNS to UEI) and a PRA-statement update. It did not address the abandonment of the proposed Part 121 / Fair Housing Act expansion.

5.4. OIRA approved the narrowed 2023 ICR on June 13, 2023, without any public-record explanation for the abandonment.

5.5. The Secretary has thus not stated, on the public record, a current determination under 24 CFR Part 121 as to whether data on disability, family characteristics, or other protected-class categories beyond race and ethnicity is "necessary or appropriate" for HUD to discharge its Fair Housing Act and related responsibilities.

5.6. The consequence for individuals with disabilities, including this Petitioner, is a public informational environment in which HUD itself does not aggregate and publish disability-status data on HUD-assisted populations, and in which Part 8 monitoring is accordingly degraded.

5.7. Three 2026 federal actions frame the legal landscape in which this Petition will be decided, and Petitioner requests that each be made part of the administrative record. On June 18, 2026, the Department of Justice's Office of Legal Counsel concluded, in the context of state institutionalization of persons with mental disabilities, that the HHS and DOJ integration regulations exceed statutory authority. *Application of the Rehabilitation Act and Americans with Disabilities Act to State Institutionalization of Patients with Severe Mental Illness or Disabilities*, 50 Op. O.L.C. __ (June 18, 2026). On July 20, 2026, DOJ declared its *Olmstead* enforcement guidance "not enforceable" and stated that it will not rely on that guidance in Title II enforcement. 91 Fed. Reg. 45,287 (July 20, 2026). On July 21, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission voted to propose rescinding the EEO-1 through EEO-6 reports; the proposal published as Removal of Reporting Requirements, 91 Fed. Reg. 46,332 (proposed July 23, 2026) (RIN 3046-AB37; to be codified at 29 C.F.R. pt. 1602; comments due August 24, 2026). The proposal rescinds the recordkeeping and record-preservation requirements tied to those reports while expressly retaining the general record-preservation requirements, 29 C.F.R. §§ 1602.14, 1602.31, 1602.40, whose retained text preserves requests for reasonable accommodation. 91 Fed. Reg. at 46,334 & n.5, 46,347. The comment period on that proposal closes August 24, 2026, and the rescission is a proposal unless and until the Commission takes final action; this Petition describes it as proposed throughout. Confirming its status, citation, and covered report series against the published text before filing is a term of [`./ADAPTATION_CHECKLIST.md`](./ADAPTATION_CHECKLIST.md), Part B.2.

5.8. None of those actions addresses this Petition's object, and none diminishes it. The OLC opinion analyzes neither the Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. § 3614a, 42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6), nor 24 CFR Part 8; the modules requested in Section 6.1 record the administration of duties whose existence no federal action questions, and no module depends on the contested integration mandate. The EEOC's proposal targets recurring demographic reports resting on Title VII's permissive reporting text, under which employers report only "as the Commission shall prescribe by regulation or order," 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-8(c); § 3608(e)(6), by contrast, commands the Secretary to report annually and to collect the information necessary or appropriate to do so. A record of a regulated transaction is not a recurring demographic report, and the two need not share a legal fate.

## 6. Actions requested

Petitioner respectfully requests that the Secretary take one or more of the following actions:

### 6.1. Adopt a companion minimum-record rule (four severable modules)

Initiate a rulemaking under 5 U.S.C. § 553(b) to adopt a defined minimum-record rule for HUD-assisted programs — an amendment to 24 CFR Part 8 and the applicable program regulations, or a stand-alone companion regulation under 42 U.S.C. § 3614a and 24 CFR Part 121 — comprising four expressly severable modules:

- **Module 1 (transaction records).** Covered PHAs and recipients preserve a dual-source record of each accommodation or modification transaction that the provider classifies as disability-related or the requester so designates — date and channel of receipt, responsible office, request category, action status, response and disposition dates, disposition category, and the stated reason where one is recorded or independently required — and provide the requester a contemporaneous accessible copy. No form or phrasing is required of the requester; an unlogged request supports no adverse inference; a facially compliant log is not a defense to any claim.
- **Module 2 (asset records).** Covered recipients maintain accessible-unit inventory and program-compliance records: unit count, accessibility type and governing standard, bedroom size, verification source and date, and allocation status.
- **Module 3 (aggregate reporting).** HUD collects and publishes PRA-cleared aggregate counts, dispositions, and timing measures implementing 42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6)'s annual reporting command, with small-cell suppression and no person-level disclosure.
- **Module 4 (restricted matching; most readily severed).** At HUD's option, regulator-only unit-to-household matching records maintained under Privacy Act system-of-records treatment. HUD may decline this module entirely while granting the others.

Each module is severable, and a grant of Module 1 alone would materially advance the Petition's object. Implementation vehicles — program form, recurring 24 CFR 8.55(b) compliance reports, PRA-cleared collection or pilot, contract term, guidance, or rulemaking — are left to HUD's choice, and a lower-burden vehicle that performs a module's function narrows the dispute.

### 6.2. Initiate broader civil-rights data rulemaking (alternative)

In the alternative, initiate a rulemaking under 5 U.S.C. § 553(b) to (a) amend 24 CFR Part 121 to specify the categories of data HUD will require of its program participants for civil-rights monitoring, or (b) amend HUD's Title VI and Fair Housing Act implementing regulations to formalize a broader standardized civil-rights data-collection instrument beyond Form HUD-27061's current race-and-ethnicity scope.

### 6.3. Modify the approved 2023 ICR

In the alternative, modify the approved 2023 ICR (202301-2535-001) under 44 U.S.C. § 3507(i), which permits OMB-approved modifications of approved collections, to expand Form HUD-27061 to include disability, family-characteristics, and other protected-class categories consistent with 42 U.S.C. § 3608(e)(6) and 24 CFR Part 121.

### 6.4. Issue a reasoned explanation on the record

In the further alternative, issue a written determination, published in the Federal Register, stating the Secretary's current determination under 24 CFR Part 121 as to whether collection of disability, family-characteristics, and other protected-class data is "necessary or appropriate" for HUD to discharge its Fair Housing Act and related responsibilities, and the Secretary's reasons for that determination. This alternative satisfies the reasoned-explanation obligation under *FCC v. Fox Television Stations, Inc.*, 556 U.S. 502 (2009), and *Motor Vehicle Mfrs. Ass'n v. State Farm*, 463 U.S. 29 (1983), for HUD's departure from the scope it had proposed in the 2022 60-day notice. *See also Department of Commerce v. New York*, 588 U.S. 752 (2019) (APA reasoned-decisionmaking applied to an agency information-collection instrument).

### 6.5. Respond within a reasonable time

In any event, under 5 U.S.C. § 555(b), respond to this Petition within a reasonable time — not to exceed 180 days from the date of submission — with a written decision granting, denying, or otherwise disposing of the Petition, including a statement of the grounds for the disposition.

## 9. Severability and anticipated objections

9.1. The four modules requested in Section 6.1 are expressly severable, and Petitioner requests module-specific disposition: a ground for denying one module is not a ground for denying another, and a disposition that treats the four modules as one undifferentiated "data" project would not be responsive to the Petition presented.

9.2. *If HUD invokes the reasoning of the June 2026 OLC opinion* — that Section 504 requires evenhanded treatment rather than affirmative obligations — the disposition should explain why that reasoning reaches recordkeeping at all. The modules impose no substantive obligation, alter no liability standard, and rest on authorities the opinion does not analyze: 42 U.S.C. §§ 3604(f)(3), 3608(e)(6), and 3614a, Section 504's express rulemaking command, and HUD's program-contract authority. No module depends on 24 CFR 8.4(d) or on any general integration mandate.

9.3. *If HUD invokes burden, tailoring, or classification concerns of the kind advanced in the EEOC's 2026 rescission proposal*, the disposition should distinguish the modules. Modules 1, 2, and 4 preserve events, assets, and restricted program records — not recurring demographic disclosures — and Module 3 implements a statute that, unlike Title VII's permissive reporting text, commands annual reporting. The EEOC proposal itself draws this line: it expressly retains the general record-preservation requirements, 29 C.F.R. §§ 1602.14, 1602.31, 1602.40 — provisions whose retained text reaches requests for reasonable accommodation — while rescinding only the report-tied collections. 91 Fed. Reg. at 46,334 & n.5, 46,347. Even the rescission proposal treats transaction-level record preservation as a different object from recurring demographic reporting. A reasoned denial should also address the tiered, suppressed, diagnosis-free design requested in Section 6.1 on its own terms.

9.4. *If HUD prefers an incremental vehicle* — a pilot, guidance revision, program-form or contract revision, or recurring 24 CFR 8.55(b) reports — a response performing a module's function would narrow the dispute, and Petitioner invites it as a partial grant.
