Supporting Statement for Paperwork Reduction Act Submissions

HUD Standardized Grant Application Form HUD 27061

(OMB # 2535-0113)

A. Justification

1.  Through 42 USC 3608e(6) the Secretary of HUD is required to annually
    report to congress and make available to the public data on the
    characteristics of persons and households who participate in
    programs administered by the Department. This provision expressly
    permits the Secretary to collect such information relating to those
    characteristics as the Secretary deems appropriate.

  42 U.S.C. 3608(e)(6): “annually report to the Congress, and make
  available to the public, data on the race, color, religion, sex,
  national origin, age, handicap, and family characteristics of persons
  and households who are applicants for, participants in, or
  beneficiaries or potential beneficiaries of, programs administered by
  the Department to the extent such characteristics are within the
  coverage of the provisions of law and Executive orders referred to in
  subsection (f) of this section which apply to such programs (and in
  order to develop the data to be included and made available to the
  public under this subsection, the Secretary shall, without regard to
  any other provision of law, collect such information relating to those
  characteristics as the Secretary determines to be necessary or
  appropriate).”

  The purpose of this part is to enable the Secretary of Housing and
  Urban Development to carry out his or her responsibilities under the
  Fair Housing Act (42 USC 3608(a)), Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of
  1964 (42 USC 2000d), and section 562 of the Housing and Community
  Development Act of 1987 and the Executive Orders 12892, 11063. These
  authorities prohibit discrimination in housing and in programs
  receiving federal financial assistance and direct the Department to
  administer its programs and activities in a manner affirmatively to
  further fair housing.

2.  This is a reinstatement with change of a currently approved
    collection. The information collection is required to ensure HUD’s
    compliance with the Fair Housing Act, Title VI of the Civil Rights
    Act of 1964, and implementation of OMB’s 1997 Statistical Policy
    Directive No. 15: Standards for Maintaining, Collecting, and
    Presenting Federal Data on Race and Ethnicity (SPD 15). HUD’s method
    for electronic collection of form HUD 27061 standardizes the format
    for grant-level information collection requirements for both
    applicants and recipients of HUD funding, and is consistent with the
    minimum data requirements in SPD 15. To ensure that all programs
    comply with Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the form is
    submitted annually, either with the application or once per year
    during the project period.

  The form allows all applicants and recipients of HUD funding, which
  include public housing authorities, State and local units of
  government, non-profit and for-profit organizations, and tribal
  entities, to report expected and actual grant-level race and ethnic
  data. Forms that report actual grant-level data typically represent a
  summation of individual household data reported during the intake
  process, either by the head of household or by observation.

  Given the wide variation in HUD programs utilizing the information
  collection, respondent organizations collect and report this
  information in various ways. For example, a program that distributes
  assistance to individual end-recipients through applications, such as
  homeowners, would be able to collect the information included in the
  collection through the application. However, a HUD program funding
  organizations that provide wide-spread services, or activities that
  may not have an identifiable pool of end-recipients, and may need to
  rely on census data in their service areas to estimate the population
  served. Furthermore, the interaction with the end-recipient varies,
  and can be a self-identification by the end-recipient, use of census
  data, or proxy identification when necessary, in line with OMB’s SPD
  15.

  Given the wide variation in HUD programs utilizing the information
  collection, recipient organizations may submit form information in
  different ways. Data collected may be submitted annual through
  program-specific electronic systems, which include: grants.gov,
  GrantSolutions, esnaps, DRGR, PIC. But all data collected must be kept
  in the recipients’ program files and may be collected during a Federal
  audit or monitoring event.

  The standardization of this collection allows HUD to analyze the data
  collected across grants and across HUD programs. Also, data reported
  will be used by HUD to determine compliance with civil rights laws.
  The form is a mandatory submission for all HUD programs not excepted
  by the requirements of the Fair Housing Act and the Civil Rights Act
  of 1964.

  While this collection seeks to reinstate the existing form with
  minimal changes, drawbacks in data quality, including statistical
  errors inherent in OMB’s SPD 15, and easy of usability are not
  addressed. HUD is working with OMB and other Federal agencies to
  update OMB’s SPD 15 and improve the data quality and usability of the
  data collection. These changes will be reflected in the next renewal
  of this data collection.

3.  This form is downloadable from Grants.gov and www.hud.gov, in both
    Microsoft Word and PDF formats. Applicants are made aware of the
    location of the form within the NOFO and recipients are made aware
    in the award terms and conditions.

4.  The form uniformly collects information across applicants and
    funding years. The collection is not duplicative.

5.  This collection does not have a significant economic impact on small
    businesses or entities.

6.  If form content was reduced, HUD staff’s ability to comply with the
    Fair Housing Act and Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 could
    be impaired. The information reported on the form is part of the
    accountability mandated by Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964,
    with respect to HUD assistance made available and awarded.

7.  There are no special circumstances required in the collection of the
    information. The following circumstances do not apply to this
    collection:

- requiring respondents to report information to the agency more than
  quarterly; N/A

- requiring respondents to prepare a written response to a collection of
  information in fewer than 30 days after receipt of it; N/A

- requiring respondents to submit more than an original and two copies
  of any document; N/A

- requiring respondents to retain records other than health, medical,
  government contract, grant-in-aid, or tax records for more than three
  years; N/A

- in connection with a statistical survey, that is not designed to
  produce valid and reliable results than can be generalized to the
  universe of study; N/A

- requiring the use of a statistical data classification that has not
  been reviewed and approved by OMB; N/A

- that includes a pledge of confidentiality that is not supported by
  authority established in statute or regulation, that is not supported
  by disclosure and data security policies that are consistent with the
  pledge, or which unnecessarily impedes sharing of data with other
  agencies for compatible confidential use; or N/A

- requiring respondents to submit proprietary trade secret, or other
  confidential information unless the agency can demonstrate that it has
  instituted procedures to protect the information's confidentiality to
  the extent permitted by law. N/A

8.  This information is collected in a manner consistent with guidelines
    of 5 CFR 1320.8(d). The Notice Announcing the collection of
    information appeared in the Federal Register on Tuesday, September
    27, 2022, at 87 FR 58524. Three comments were received; however,
    none caused a change in the form.

  One of the three comments received requested HUD add sexual
  orientation and gender identity (SOGI) questions to both Form 27061
  and HUD Form 27061-H. The comment stated LGBTQ+ older adults face
  significant barriers in receiving services and securing housing and
  highlighted a growing trend among other agencies in adding LGBTQ+ data
  collection. The comment also identified HUD’s obligation to collect
  data concerning the sex of applicants, participants, and beneficiaries
  of HUD programs for purposes of enforcing the Fair Housing Act and the
  Civil Rights Act of 1964, which prohibit discrimination on the basis
  of sex, including sexual orientation and gender identity. The
  commenter identified three additional questions for Form 27061-H that
  would then be compiled on Form 27061: (1) applicant’s sex assigned at
  birth, (2) which gender they identify as now, and (3) their sexual
  orientation.

  HUD appreciates the suggestions of the comment and remains interested
  in collecting SOGI data to inform enforcement of civil right statutes
  under HUD’s jurisdiction. However, HUD is not adding the SOGI
  questions during this renewal process, which applies only to Form
  27061. While HUD agrees that collecting SOGI data would prove helpful
  in expanding and preserving protections for LGBTQ+ individuals in HUD
  programs and housing, HUD remains concerned that the process of
  applicants asking LGBTQ+ individuals to disclose their gender and
  sexual identity and then aggregating that data in the 27061 could be
  problematic. While the commenter stated that social stigma no longer
  prevents most LGBTQ+ persons from answering survey questions about
  their sexual orientation or gender identity, HUD has concerns about
  collecting this data for Form 27061. However, HUD appreciates the
  comment and notes that it is intended to apply to other data
  collections HUD undertakes and will explore other opportunities to
  collect SOGI data. In particular, when Form 27061-H undergoes PRA
  renewal, among other HUD forms, HUD will evaluate inclusion of SOGI
  data collection, among other protected characteristics, to inform its
  grantmaking and oversight.

  The other two comments requested a copy of the existing form. The
  current data elements for the form remains unchanged and is available
  on HUD’s website at
  https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/OCHCO/documents/27061.pdf, as noted
  in the 60-day notice published in the Federal Register.

9.  No payments or gifts are provided to respondents.

10. There is no assurance of confidentiality provided to the
    respondents. The form collects grant-level summary information,
    which does not include personal identifying information. The
    information requested under this collection is protected and held
    private in accordance with 5 U.S.C. 552 (Freedom of Information
    Act), and (Privacy Act of 1974).

11. The collection does not contain information of a sensitive nature
    about sexual behavior or attitudes, religious beliefs, or private
    matters.

16. The information may be made available publicly depending on requests
    from OMB, Congress, or Federal agencies with appropriate authority.
    Also, individual HUD programs may assess the need to publish
    information. When this information is used to inform public reports
    or is otherwise reported publicly, HUD will ensure adequate
    discussion is included regarding validity concerns and biases in how
    this data is collected by grantee organizations and aggregated by
    HUD. This discussion should acknowledge certain limitations to this
    dataset including: (1) the inability for grantee organizations to
    report non-responses or refused response data and a lack of clarity
    as to how these data-points are reported to HUD; (2) potentially
    varied collection methods, including both self-identification and
    proxy reporting (which may present validity issues); (3) that
    entities do not report multiracial categories that are below 1% of
    all respondents, which may inadvertently skew the aggregate
    reporting of the racial and/or ethnic identity of individuals served
    by HUD grantees. Following internal consultation, the agency may
    also choose to discuss additional sources of error or bias. .

17. The information collection instruments will display the expiration
    date for OMB approval of the information collection.

18. There are no exceptions to the certification statement identified in
    item 19 of the OMB 83-I.
