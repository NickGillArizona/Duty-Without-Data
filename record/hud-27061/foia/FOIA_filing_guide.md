# FOIA Filing Guide

## Research Project: HUD Form 27061 / OMB Control No. 2535-0113

**Prepared:** 2026-04-18

This guide accompanies the three FOIA requests drafted in this directory. It explains (a) what each request seeks and why, (b) how to file, (c) expected timelines, and (d) what to do when responses arrive (or do not).

---

## 1. What each FOIA seeks and why it matters to the note

### FOIA 1 — HUD Pre-2006 Records (historical completeness)

**File:** `FOIA_1_HUD_pre2006_records.md`

**Targets:** HUD (Executive Secretariat FOIA Branch)

**Seeks:** The Supporting Statements, Federal Register submission packages, and OIRA clearance correspondence for the 2003 ICR cycles (OIRA refs 200302-2535-001 and 200308-2535-001). These cycles pre-date RegInfo.gov's reliable document attachment regime and are not publicly accessible.

**Why it matters:** Completes the longitudinal record back to the initial 2003 adoption of Form HUD-27061. Allows the note to anchor its 20-year analysis in the original scope and justification, rather than stopping at the 2006 cycle.

**Litigation value:** Moderate. Primarily historical context; unlikely to contain decisive material. Lowest priority of the three.

**Expected response:** Likely substantive. 2003 records should not invoke Exemption 5 (deliberative privilege has a time-and-finality dimension), and the cycles long pre-date the 2022-2023 reversion that is the core of the claim.

---

### FOIA 2 — HUD Internal Communications (the "why" behind the reversion)

**File:** `FOIA_2_HUD_internal_communications.md`

**Targets:** HUD (Executive Secretariat FOIA Branch)

**Seeks:** All internal HUD communications, memoranda, drafts, and messages between August 1, 2022 and March 31, 2023 concerning Form HUD-27061, the 60-day notice, the 30-day notice, the SAGE/Jones Day comment, the proposed 24 CFR 121 expansion, or the title/scope change.

**Why it matters:** The public record establishes the reversion but is silent on the agency's rationale. This FOIA is the only path to the internal decisional record. **This is the most important of the three.**

**Litigation value:** Very high. Any document in the responsive corpus that (a) acknowledges the substantive disability-data gap the 60-day expansion would have closed and nonetheless directs scope narrowing, or (b) reveals political or external pressure for the reversion, is the evidentiary core of a State Farm claim.

**Expected response:** HUD will invoke FOIA Exemption 5 (deliberative process privilege) aggressively. Expect significant redactions. Exemption 5 does not, however, protect:

- **Post-decisional records** — communications after the January 27, 2023 publication of the 30-day notice that explain or justify the decision already made (these become the agency's "working law");
- **Purely factual records** — data, analysis, citations, and other non-opinion content;
- **Pre-decisional records adopted into final decision** — a memo that the agency actually adopted as its reasoning may lose deliberative protection.

Be prepared to administratively appeal any over-broad Exemption 5 claims and, if necessary, litigate under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(B).

---

### FOIA 3 — OMB/OIRA Pass-back Correspondence (the interagency angle)

**File:** `FOIA_3_OMB_OIRA_passback.md`

**Targets:** Office of Management and Budget / OIRA

**Seeks:** OIRA desk officer notes, terms of clearance, pass-back memoranda, and correspondence with HUD concerning the 202301-2535-001 cycle between September 1, 2022 and July 31, 2023.

**Why it matters:** OIRA is HUD's reviewer under the Paperwork Reduction Act. If OIRA pushed HUD to narrow scope, or if OIRA internally flagged any concerns about the 60-day expansion that HUD later accommodated, the evidentiary picture shifts: the narrowing may be attributable to OIRA pressure rather than HUD independent judgment. Either way, the OIRA record is a separate witness on the decision.

**Litigation value:** Medium-to-high. OIRA pass-back memoranda sometimes contain frank assessments that do not appear in any public HUD record.

**Expected response:** OMB/OIRA is more restrictive than HUD on FOIA productions and invokes Exemption 5 broadly. Plan for a long fight and administrative appeal.

## 2. How to file

### Recommended path: FOIA.gov portal

1. Go to **https://www.foia.gov**
2. Create an account (quick, free).
3. For FOIA 1 and FOIA 2: select **"Department of Housing and Urban Development"** as the agency.
4. For FOIA 3: select **"Office of Management and Budget."**
5. Paste the body of the request into the portal's request-description field. Attach the `.md` file as a supplemental document if the portal allows attachments. (Some users prefer to paste only a short summary in the field and upload the full request as a PDF — either works.)
6. Set "Fee waiver requested: Yes" and "Expedited processing requested: Yes" (for FOIAs 2 and 3) where the portal prompts.
7. Retain the tracking number returned by the portal — you will need it for every follow-up and for an administrative appeal.

### Alternative path: Direct email

- **HUD:** foia@hud.gov — subject line "FOIA Request — [SHORT DESCRIPTION] — [YOUR LAST NAME]"
- **OMB:** OMBFOIA@omb.eop.gov — same subject convention

Attach the `.md` file converted to PDF. A PDF version is appended to the email body inline for robustness.

### Alternative path: Postal mail

Use the addresses at the top of each request. Certified mail with return receipt is recommended for record purposes, but is slower than electronic filing.

### Before filing — fill in every bracket

Every `[YOUR ...]` placeholder in each request must be replaced with your actual information. Do a text-find for the bracket character `[` to make sure none remain. The FOIA requests are drafted in first-person singular as if filed by you personally; they are not drafted as attorney submissions.

## 3. Expected timelines

| Stage | Statutory deadline | Realistic timing |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledgment of receipt | 10 working days (5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(A)(i)) | 1–3 weeks |
| Decision on expedited processing | 10 calendar days (5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(E)(ii)(I)) | Often late |
| Initial substantive response | 20 working days (5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(A)) | 2–12+ months |
| Appeal decision after denial | 20 working days | 2–6 months |
| Litigation option | After "constructive exhaustion" (agency unresponsive for statutory period) | Counsel's decision |

**Practical reality:** FOIA deadlines are routinely missed. Plan on six to twelve months for substantive production on FOIA 2; FOIA 1 may be faster. Build the note so that the public-record evidence already in `./record/hud-27061/` is sufficient to support publication, and treat FOIA productions as enrichment for future work or a litigation filing.

## 4. What to do when responses arrive

### Acknowledgment letter
File it. Note the tracking number, the assigned FOIA analyst's name/email, and any stated deadline.

### "Unusual circumstances" extension letter
Common. Not a denial. Agencies invoke 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(B) to extend by 10 working days. Acknowledge receipt and confirm the request remains active.

### Interim production
Catalog every released document. Update the methodology memo's file inventory (`file_inventory.csv`) with a new row for each document, including its FOIA tracking number and the date of release.

### Withholding / redaction
For each redacted page or withheld document, note:
1. The exemption cited (e.g., "Exemption 5");
2. The page or document number;
3. Whether any reasoning for the exemption is given;
4. Whether a Vaughn-style index is provided.

If the exemption appears over-broad or unexplained, consider an administrative appeal. HUD appeals go to the HUD FOIA Appeals Officer; OMB appeals go to the OMB FOIA Appeals Officer. Each must be filed within 90 days of the denial.

### Complete denial
File an administrative appeal. Do not skip this step — exhaustion of administrative remedies is a prerequisite to FOIA litigation under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(B).

### No response at all
If the agency fails to respond within the statutory period (20 working days plus any properly-invoked 10-day extension), "constructive exhaustion" applies under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(C)(i). At that point, judicial review becomes available.

## 5. Privacy considerations

FOIA requests themselves become part of a public record and are sometimes posted online (e.g., through MuckRock, if the requester chooses to use that service as a submission platform; or through an agency's reading room). The request letters draft in this directory identify the author as a JD candidate and disclose contact information. If anonymity is preferred, consider:

- Filing via a FOIA clearinghouse service (e.g., MuckRock) that can accept the email on the requester's behalf;
- Using a dedicated email address;
- Using the law school's mailing address rather than a residential one.

None of these affect the substantive requests.

## 6. Staggered filing strategy (recommended)

Rather than filing all three requests on the same day, consider the following sequence:

**Week 1:** File FOIA 2 (HUD internal communications). Highest priority; longest expected lead time.

**Week 2:** File FOIA 3 (OMB/OIRA pass-back). Independent custodian; don't let HUD's processing queue slow the OMB clock.

**Week 4:** File FOIA 1 (HUD pre-2006 records). Lowest priority; file last so the HUD FOIA office sees the others as part of a connected research program rather than a single large request.

Staggering also distributes the fee-waiver evaluation burden on the agencies and may produce better outcomes than a batch submission.

## 7. Appeals and litigation

If an agency denies any portion of a request or fails to respond within the statutory deadline:

1. **File an administrative appeal** within 90 days. The appeal letter should identify the request, the tracking number, the specific portions denied, and the grounds for challenge. It should be filed with the agency's FOIA Appeals Officer (address varies by agency).
2. If the appeal is denied or not resolved within 20 working days, **"constructive exhaustion" attaches.**
3. **Litigation** under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(B) may be filed in (a) the U.S. District Court for the District where the requester resides; (b) the District where records are located; or (c) the District of Columbia. Litigation is counsel's responsibility — a JD candidate acting pro se is permitted but is a practical risk. Consider referring a successful FOIA appeal fight to a FOIA clinic, a public-interest firm, or a reporter's committee for partnership.
