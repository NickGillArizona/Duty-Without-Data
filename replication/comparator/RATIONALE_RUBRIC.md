# Common-Rubric Pleading-Rationale Taxonomy

Assurance: EXTENDED (machine-classified; verified by an independent AI verification run, not a manual hand-count).

## Assignment Logic

The A/B/C family assignment is class-neutral. Coders must first classify the missing allegation or defect by the kind of fact or legal defect the court identified, without relying on protected class.

## Family A: Institutionally-Held-Fact Deficit

The missing allegation is a fact the defendant institution would ordinarily record or control: request and response, dates, internal decisions, unit or building facts, file status, responsible actor, notice, denial, or timing.

Subcodes:

- Disability subcodes: notice, nexus, timing, denial, covered_status.
- Race subcodes: intent, comparator, adverse_action, causation.

## Family B: Plaintiff-Known-Fact Deficit

The missing allegation is primarily a fact within the plaintiff's own ordinary knowledge: what happened, who said what, where an incident occurred, what conduct the plaintiff personally experienced, or facts supporting intent or comparator treatment that do not depend on institutional records.

Subcodes:

- Disability subcodes: personal_incident_detail, subjective_need, alleged_harm.
- Race subcodes: intent, comparator, adverse_action, causation.

## Family C: Legal Insufficiency Independent Of Fact Detail

The defect would remain even with more factual detail: wrong defendant, non-dwelling, no cognizable claim, limitations, standing, jurisdiction, immunity, claim preclusion, or a statute/regulation that does not reach the alleged conduct.

## Masking Rule

Before coding, protected-class identifiers and claim-type giveaways should be masked where feasible. Accommodation language is inherently leaky because the claim type itself often identifies disability; masking leakage must be reported and the result stays at RESEARCH LEAD assurance until human review.
