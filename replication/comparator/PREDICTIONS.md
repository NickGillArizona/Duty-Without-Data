## Mandatory Peek Disclosure

on 2026-07-07, during feasibility checking and BEFORE this work order runs, the author computed crude, unstratified plaintiff-win counts by bucket x period (RD-PURE roughly 29% P1 -> 11% P3; DT-PURE roughly 10% -> 15%; no representation stratification, no CIs, machine outcomes unadjudicated).

This registration uses that disclosure as a known feasibility peek. The predictions below are registered over quantities not yet computed in this run: representation-stratified rates, decomposition shares, interaction terms, and rationale-mix distributions.

## Predictions

1. Pro se share rises in the race cohort from P1 to P3, but by less than the disability cohort's rise.
2. The aggregate strict-win decline is steeper in disability than race, and the representation-composition share of the P1-to-P3 decline is larger in disability.
3. Within disability, RD-PURE has a steeper strict-win decline and a stronger pleading-gate concentration than DT-PURE; RD-PURE movement is substantially compositional, while any DT-PURE movement is not.
4. Under the common class-neutral rubric, institutionally-held-fact deficits are overrepresented in RD-PURE pro se pleading losses relative to both DT-PURE disability and race pro se pleading losses.
5. Race disparate-treatment merits outcomes are comparatively stable across periods.

## What Would Count Against The Thesis

The following results would weaken the record-dependence sharpening and must be reported as such:

- Identical rationale mixes across RD-PURE, DT-PURE, and RACE-DT pleading-loss arms.
- A race representation-composition share equal to or larger than disability's.
- A DT-PURE decline matching RD-PURE after representation stratification.
- A rationale mix showing DT-PURE pro se losses dominated by institutionally-held-fact deficits without a corresponding translation-problem interpretation.
- Race disparate-treatment merits outcomes showing a sharper period decline than disability without a plausible class-specific shock explanation.
